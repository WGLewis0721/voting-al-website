Candidate Comparison

People, priorities, and likely outcomes.

Open each card for “If they win” context. This guide is educational and nonpartisan.

Governor · Republican

Tommy Tuberville

U.S. Senator; America First profile.

  • Border enforcement
  • Conservative judges
  • Tax and business focus
State policy likely remains deeply conservative with tight federal alignment.
Why this matters: Affects healthcare expansion, education spending, and court direction.

Governor · Democrat

Doug Jones

Former U.S. Senator; reform-oriented profile.

  • Medicaid expansion
  • Public school investment
  • Workforce programs
Would likely push healthcare and education investment while negotiating with a GOP-majority legislature.
Why this matters: Could widen access to care but depends heavily on legislative support.

U.S. Senate · Republican

Barry Moore

U.S. Representative; Trump-endorsed.

  • Conservative federal votes
  • Strong border policy
  • Gun rights focus
Expected to align tightly with conservative national priorities.
Why this matters: Influences federal judges, spending bills, and immigration law.

U.S. Senate · Republican

Steve Marshall

Current Alabama Attorney General.

  • States-rights legal strategy
  • Pro-life platform
  • Agency oversight
Likely to bring an AG-style legal posture into Senate policy priorities.
Why this matters: Shapes legal conflicts and confirmation decisions with long-term impact.

U.S. Senate · Republican

Jared Hudson

Navy veteran; outsider campaign.

  • Immigration enforcement
  • Defense and veterans lens
  • Federal spending cuts
Would enter with less Senate seniority but a strong outsider message.
Why this matters: Committee influence starts lower, but major votes still directly affect Alabama.

Down-ballot Lens

Your County Candidates

School board, sheriff, and commission races often affect daily life fastest.

  • School direction
  • Public safety approach
  • Local service funding
Local races can be decided by very small margins and produce immediate policy effects.
Why this matters: These offices often impact your day-to-day life sooner than statewide races.
