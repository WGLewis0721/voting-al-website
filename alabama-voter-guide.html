What's On the Ballot

Statewide ballot measures — plain English

Alabama voters don't put things on the ballot — the legislature does. A simple majority decides each one. Here's every certified statewide measure for 2026.

A1
May 19 Primary
Expanding When Judges Can Deny Bail

Adds two new crime categories — shooting in an occupied space and murder conspiracy — to the list where judges can hold someone in jail before trial.

YES means
Judges gain new power to deny bail for these crimes before any conviction
NO means
Bail rules stay as they are — no new restrictions
In Plain English

Right now, judges can deny bail only for specific serious crimes like capital murder. This amendment adds firing a gun inside an occupied building or vehicle, and planning, attempting, or hiring someone to commit murder, to that list.

Why It Affects Alabama Families

Bail determines whether an accused person goes home or stays in jail until trial. Expanding no-bail crimes may reduce reoffending while awaiting trial — but it also means more people held before being convicted of anything. County jails (funded locally) may see more population pressure. Alabama already passed "Aniah's Law" in 2020 for similar reasons; this extends that approach.

A2
May 19 Primary
Protecting District Attorney Pay

Makes it unconstitutional to cut a district attorney's salary while they're serving their elected term in office.

YES means
DA pay is protected — they can prosecute without fear of budget retaliation
NO means
Legislature keeps ability to adjust DA pay — some accountability is maintained
In Plain English

District attorneys decide which crimes get prosecuted — from drug offenses to violent crime. Alabama judges already have this pay protection. This gives DAs the same guarantee.

Why It Affects Alabama Families

Pay protection means a DA can make prosecution decisions based on evidence — not fear of political retaliation. Supporters say it ensures independent justice. Critics say it reduces accountability. Either way, it shapes how aggressively crime is prosecuted in your county.

WC
Walker County Only
Probate Judge Retirement Age: 70 → 75

Walker County voters only. Allows the probate judge to serve five additional years before mandatory retirement.

YES means
Experienced judges can serve until 75 instead of retiring at 70
NO means
Mandatory retirement stays at age 70 as it is today
In Plain English — Walker County Voters Only

The probate judge oversees wills, estates, mental health commitment hearings, and county government administration. This simply raises the mandatory retirement age from 70 to 75 for that role. If you don't live in Walker County, this measure doesn't appear on your ballot.

Your Questions

Straight answers.
No cheerleading.

These aren't complaints — they're the real questions voters are asking. Here's our honest take.

Is this just about political power and positioning — not really for the people?

Sometimes, yes. Political ambition is real. That's exactly why we show you what candidates have actually done in office — not what their party says. Voting records and legal actions are public. You can judge for yourself.

I don't know who to vote for.

Start with one issue that affects your daily life — school quality, healthcare costs, crime, jobs, taxes. Use the filter bar above to see which candidates focus on that issue. You don't need to agree on everything. Vote on what matters most to you.

My vote doesn't change anything.

Alabama's 2017 U.S. Senate race was decided by about 21,000 votes statewide. School board and county races are decided by hundreds. Your vote is most powerful in primaries — fewer people show up, so each one counts more.

I don't know what's going on.

That's what this page is for. You don't need to follow politics daily. Find one issue affecting your life, see where candidates stand on it, and go vote. That's all it takes for an informed ballot.

Why does the governor or senator matter to me personally?

The governor controls Alabama's ~$14 billion budget — your school's funding, road repairs, Medicaid, prison policy. Your senator votes on healthcare, student loans, military spending, and who joins the Supreme Court for life.

Why does the primary matter if the general is already decided?

In deep-red Alabama, the Republican primary winner often becomes the officeholder. A smaller group of primary voters effectively decides who governs everyone. Your vote also shapes local races and state resources flowing to your county.

How to Vote

Five steps.
No jargon.

Everything you need to vote in Alabama's 2026 elections.

1

Check if you're registered

You must register at least 14 days before any election. For May 19, the deadline is May 4, 2026. Moved recently — even within the same county? Update your registration or you'll be redirected to the wrong polling place.

Check or Update Registration →
2

Bring a valid photo ID — required by Alabama law

You must show one of these at the polls:

Alabama Driver's License Alabama Non-Driver ID U.S. Passport U.S. Military ID State / Federal Employee ID Valid Tribal ID

No ID? Get a free Alabama Photo Voter ID at your county Board of Registrars, or call 1-800-274-8683 for a home visit. You can also vote provisionally and show ID by the Friday after election day.

3

Find your polling place before Election Day

Your assigned polling location is based on your registered address and can change. Don't assume it's the same place as last time. Polls are open 7:00 AM – 7:00 PM.

Find My Polling Place →
4

Early voting and absentee options

Early in-person: Available starting 27 days before Election Day through the Friday before. No excuse needed — just bring your ID to an early voting location.

Absentee by mail: Only if you qualify — out of county, illness or disability, or work during all polling hours. Apply first. Return to your county Absentee Election Manager by the day before the election. Alabama has no ballot drop boxes.

5

Know which ballot you'll receive

In the primary, you choose one party's ballot — Republican or Democratic. You can't vote both. In any runoff, you must vote the same party as the primary. In the Nov 3 general election, all voters get the same ballot regardless of party.
